# KB-002 — Broadband Connectivity Troubleshooting

**Article ID:** KB-002  
**Category:** Broadband  
**Version:** 4.1  
**Effective Date:** 2023-06-01  
**Last Updated:** 2024-09-01

## Summary

Step-by-step guide for diagnosing and resolving residential and SMB broadband connectivity issues, including complete outages, intermittent connectivity, and slow speeds.

## Step 1 — Verify the Outage is Not Network-Wide

Before performing customer-side troubleshooting, check:
- Network status dashboard for the customer's area.
- Scheduled maintenance windows.
- Outage notifications for the past 24 hours.

**If a network-wide outage is confirmed:** Do NOT ask the customer to troubleshoot their equipment. Acknowledge the outage, provide ETA if available, and log the ticket.

## Step 2 — Basic Router/Modem Restart

If no network outage is confirmed, guide the customer through:

1. Unplug the router/modem from the power outlet.
2. Wait **30 seconds** (not 5 seconds — capacitors need time to discharge).
3. Plug back in.
4. Wait **2 minutes** for the router to fully initialize.
5. Check connection.

**IMPORTANT:** If the customer has already performed a restart, do NOT ask them to do so again. Proceed to Step 3.

## Step 3 — Verify Physical Connections

1. Confirm all cable connections are secure (power, LAN/WAN, coax or fiber cable).
2. If using fiber: Check the ONT (Optical Network Terminal) — all indicator lights should be solid, not blinking red.
3. If using a cable modem: Check coaxial cable is hand-tight.
4. If using DSL: Verify the phone line filter is connected.

## Step 4 — Router Diagnostic Lights

Interpret indicator lights:
- **All solid green/white:** Normal operation.
- **Internet light blinking amber:** Authentication issue — check ISP credentials.
- **Internet light solid red:** Signal loss — check physical line or call network operations.
- **Power light off:** Power issue — check outlet, adapter.
- **ONT red/orange:** Fiber signal issue — escalate to field team.

## Step 5 — Speed and Latency Test

Ask the customer to:
1. Connect a device directly via Ethernet (not Wi-Fi).
2. Visit [speedtest] or similar.
3. Note download, upload, and ping.

Compare against their subscribed plan:
- FiberMax 200: Expect 180–220 Mbps download, <10ms ping.
- FiberMax 500: Expect 450–550 Mbps download, <5ms ping.
- Fiber Basic: Expect 80–100 Mbps download, <15ms ping.

**If speed is below 80% of subscribed rate on wired connection:** Log as a service quality issue and escalate to Network team.

## Step 6 — Factory Reset (Last Resort)

**Only perform a factory reset if explicitly approved by a Tier-2 agent.**

1. Locate the RESET button on the router (usually recessed).
2. Use a pin to press and hold for 10 seconds.
3. All lights will blink and router will restart.
4. The customer must re-enter ISP credentials after reset.

**WARNING:** Factory reset erases all custom Wi-Fi settings. Customer must be informed before proceeding.

## Common Error Scenarios

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Action |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| No lights on router | Power issue | Check power adapter and outlet |
| Internet light red | No signal from ISP | Check line; escalate if persistent |
| Slow speeds on Wi-Fi only | Wi-Fi interference | Test on Ethernet; advise router placement |
| Slow speeds on all devices | Line or plan issue | Escalate to Network team |
| Intermittent drops every 30 min | Router overheating | Advise ventilation; check firmware |

## Escalation Criteria

Escalate to Tier-2 / Network team if:
- Customer has already restarted router twice with no improvement.
- Outage exceeds 4 hours (customer may be eligible for credit per KB-001).
- Speed is below 50% of subscribed rate on wired connection after restart.
- ONT shows fiber signal error.
- The issue has occurred more than 3 times in 30 days (pattern → field inspection).
- Customer reports physical damage to external cable or fiber terminal.
