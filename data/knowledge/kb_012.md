# KB-012 — Escalation Policy

**Article ID:** KB-012  
**Category:** Operations  
**Version:** 2.6  
**Effective Date:** 2024-01-01  
**Last Updated:** 2024-09-01

## Summary

Defines the escalation hierarchy, conditions for escalation, handoff requirements, and escalation SLAs.

## When to Escalate

Escalation is required when:

1. **Knowledge Gap:** The issue is not covered by any available support article and cannot be answered from available information.
2. **Contradictory Information:** Account record contradicts the customer's account of events and cannot be resolved with available data.
3. **Exceeded Authority:** The required action (refund, credit, account change) exceeds Tier-1 authority levels.
4. **Complex Multi-Issue Cases:** Multiple interdependent issues that require cross-team coordination.
5. **Repeat Issues:** Same issue reported 3+ times without permanent resolution.
6. **Potential Fraud:** Any indication of account compromise, SIM swap fraud, or unusual account activity.
7. **Business Account Requests:** SMB or enterprise accounts with SLA requirements.
8. **Customer Demand:** Customer explicitly requests to speak with a supervisor.
9. **High-Value Impact:** Billing dispute or service impact involving more than ₹2,000.

## What Must NOT Be Escalated Unnecessarily

Agents should NOT escalate routine cases that can be resolved with available policy and tools:
- Standard router restarts.
- First-time password resets.
- Basic billing inquiries where the invoice matches the account record.
- APN configuration instructions.

Unnecessary escalations waste supervisor time and worsen the customer experience. Tier-1 resolution is always preferred when possible.

## Escalation Handoff Requirements

**CRITICAL:** When escalating, the agent MUST provide a complete handoff. The customer must NEVER have to repeat their issue.

### Required Handoff Information

1. **Issue Summary:** Concise 2-3 sentence description of the core issue.
2. **Established Facts:** What has been confirmed from account records, customer statements, and system data.
3. **Customer Context:** Plan, billing status, account health, relevant history.
4. **Already Attempted:** Every action already taken in the session — steps tried, checks performed.
5. **Contradictions Found:** Any conflicts between customer statements and system records.
6. **Evidence:** System data, ticket history, relevant KB articles consulted.
7. **Why Escalation:** Clear reason why Tier-1 cannot resolve.
8. **Recommended Next Action:** What the Tier-2 agent should investigate or do first.

### Escalation Format

The handoff must be structured and written, not verbal. Use the standard escalation template in the case management system.

## Escalation Tiers

| Tier | Handles |
|---|---|
| Tier-1 | Standard support, first-line troubleshooting |
| Tier-2 | Complex billing, plan exceptions, technical escalations |
| Network Ops | Infrastructure issues, outages, speed problems |
| Security Team | Fraud, SIM swap, identity theft, account compromise |
| Finance Team | Refunds >₹5,000, billing disputes >₹2,000 |
| Executive Escalation | Business accounts, media escalations, high-value customers |

## Escalation SLAs

| Priority | Tier-2 Response | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 30 minutes | 4 hours |
| High | 2 hours | 8 hours |
| Medium | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Low | Next business day | 48 hours |
